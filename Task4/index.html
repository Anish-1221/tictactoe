Alpha-beta with three players

Minimax, and alpha-beta with it, needs two sides whose interests are exactly opposed, so that one number describes the position for both. With three players that is not true as it stands: if player 1 is ahead, players 2 and 3 are both behind, but not equally.

The fix used here is the paranoid assumption (Sturtevant and Korf, 2000). The player choosing a move assumes the other two are working together against it. That turns the game back into two sides, “me” and “everyone else”, with one number between them: my score. I maximise it on my turns, and on each opponent’s turn that opponent minimises it. Minimax applies unchanged, and so does alpha-beta pruning. The assumption is pessimistic, but it is safe: a move that holds up against two co-operating opponents holds up against two independent ones.

Which move to try first

Alpha-beta cuts more when good moves are tried early. Moves are ordered by the number of possible paths still open through the cell, meaning the lines through it that no other player has a mark in. A move that completes a line goes first, and a move that blocks another player’s line goes next. The ordering only changes the amount of work, never the value of the move chosen.

The maxn option

maxn (Luckhardt and Irani, 1986) scores every position as a vector with one value per player, and each player picks the move best for itself. It models the players more faithfully, but with no single number there is no alpha and beta to compare, so it cannot prune beyond stopping at an immediate win. Switching the search above shows what alpha-beta saves: on the same positions, maxn visited between 2 and 15 times as many nodes at depths 3 to 6.

Why equal depths draw

With three in a row to win and three players defending, every line one player starts can be blocked by either of the other two. At equal search depths every game ends drawn, in every batch at every depth from 2 to 6. Lowering one player’s depth makes it miss threats, which is what produces wins and losses for the scoreboard.
