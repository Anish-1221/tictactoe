Why this is not minimax

Minimax needs two players whose interests are exactly opposed, so a single number can describe the position for both: what helps one hurts the other by the same amount. With three players that stops holding. If player 1 is ahead then players 2 and 3 are both behind, but not equally, and neither position is the negation of player 1's.

This task uses maxn instead. Every position is scored as a vector with one component per player, and whoever is to move picks the child that maximises their own component. With two players and a zero-sum rule maxn reduces to ordinary minimax, so it is a generalisation rather than a different algorithm.

Alpha-beta does not carry over, because it depends on alpha and beta being two views of one number and there is no such pair once scores are vectors. Two sound prunes are used instead: a move that wins immediately ends the search of its siblings, and candidate moves are limited to cells near existing marks.

On the choice of win length

Four in a row is the natural rule for a 4×4 board, but with three players it is a forced draw. Sixteen cells split three ways gives each player five or six marks, and any line one player threatens can be blocked by either of the other two. Every batch run at four in a row ends drawn, at every search depth tested. Three in a row is therefore the default here, so the scoreboard shows something; switch the control to four to reproduce the drawn result.
